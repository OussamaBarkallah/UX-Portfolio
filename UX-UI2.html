Mobile App USPN Students

Overview

The USPN mobile application was designed for students of Université Sorbonne Paris Nord to improve access to academic services, campus information, and daily student life resources through a centralized mobile experience.
The project aimed to transform fragmented student interactions into a simple, intuitive, and accessible digital experience aligned with students’ everyday needs.

The Challenge

Students at Université Sorbonne Paris Nord were navigating a fragmented digital experience. Schedules, academic emails, campus services, and university announcements were scattered across multiple disconnected platforms — forcing students to constantly switch between tools, miss important updates, and lose time on tasks that should be simple. The core question we set out to answer was: how might we create an essential tool that naturally integrates into the daily life of USPN students?

My Role

Within the team, I led the UI design phase — responsible for the visual identity, component structure, and final high-fidelity screens. I also contributed to the persona definition and journey mapping sessions, and facilitated part of the ideation workshop.

Research & Methodology

To ensure our solution addressed real student needs, we followed a structured user-centered design process from the start.

Design Process

The USPN mobile app was designed to centralize academic services, campus information, and student resources into a single mobile experience. By simplifying access to essential information, the application helps students stay organized, connected, and engaged throughout their university journey.

Impact

The solution was validated during the hackathon through informal user testing with fellow students. The centralised interface received positive feedback for its clarity and ease of navigation — particularly the notification system and the schedule view, which were identified as the most immediately useful features. The project demonstrated that a single, well-structured entry point could meaningfully reduce the friction students experience in their daily academic life.
